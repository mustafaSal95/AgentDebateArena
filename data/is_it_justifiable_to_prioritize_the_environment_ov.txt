Topic: Is it justifiable to prioritize the environment over economic growth?

Research indicates that prioritizing the environment over economic growth is a viable strategy, supported by statistics on the economic benefits of environmental conservation. For instance, a study by the International Renewable Energy Agency found that transitioning to 100% renewable energy by 2050 could increase global GDP by 1% and create 24 million new jobs. Additionally, the World Wildlife Fund estimates that the global ecosystem services provided by nature, such as air and water purification, are valued at $33 trillion annually, exceeding the global GDP of $28 trillion. Conversely, the economic costs of environmental degradation are substantial, with the World Health Organization estimating that air pollution alone costs the global economy $5.7 trillion annually.

However, opponents argue that prioritizing the environment over economic growth can have significant negative impacts on low-income communities and developing economies. For example, a study by the Brookings Institution found that environmental regulations can increase energy costs, which disproportionately affect low-income households, with 20% of US households spending over 10% of their income on energy. Furthermore, the World Bank estimates that 700 million people could be pushed into poverty by 2030 due to climate change, highlighting the need for economic growth to address poverty and inequality. Nevertheless, proponents counter that a green economy can create new opportunities for sustainable development, with the International Labor Organization estimating that the green economy could create 46 million new jobs globally by 2030, and the European Union's climate and energy framework aiming to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 80% by 2050 while increasing GDP by 5%.
