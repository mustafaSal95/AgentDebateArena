Topic: Can a global treaty to ban all nuclear weapons be effective?

The Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons (TPNW), adopted by 122 countries in 2017, aims to ban all nuclear weapons. Proponents argue that a global treaty can be effective in reducing the risk of nuclear war, citing the success of the Biological Weapons Convention (BWC) and the Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC), which have led to the destruction of over 95% of declared chemical weapons and 90% of declared biological weapons. As of 2022, 59 countries have ratified the TPNW, including Austria, Ireland, and Mexico. The treaty's supporters point to the significant reduction in nuclear arsenals since the Cold War, with the total number of nuclear warheads decreasing from approximately 70,000 in 1986 to around 12,700 in 2022, according to the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI).

Despite these efforts, opponents of a global nuclear ban treaty argue that it is unrealistic and ineffective, as it does not include key nuclear-armed states such as the United States, Russia, and China. The nine nuclear-armed states possess over 13,000 nuclear warheads, with the US and Russia holding approximately 90% of the global total. Critics argue that a treaty without these countries' participation will have limited impact, citing the fact that the TPNW has not led to any significant reductions in nuclear arsenals among non-signatory states. Furthermore, the 2022 SIPRI report notes that nuclear-armed states are modernizing their arsenals, with the US planning to spend over $1.2 trillion on nuclear modernization over the next 30 years. The effectiveness of a global treaty to ban all nuclear weapons remains a topic of debate, with proponents emphasizing the moral and humanitarian imperatives, while opponents highlight the challenges of implementation and enforcement.
