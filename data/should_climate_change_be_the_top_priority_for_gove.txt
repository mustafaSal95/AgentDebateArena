Topic: Should climate change be the top priority for governments worldwide?

Climate change is a pressing global issue, with the past decade being the warmest on record, according to NASA and the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA). The concentration of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere has increased by 40% since the Industrial Revolution, reaching 415 parts per million (ppm) in 2020. Proponents of prioritizing climate change argue that immediate action is necessary to mitigate its devastating impacts, including rising sea levels, more frequent natural disasters, and loss of biodiversity. The economic costs of inaction are significant, with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) estimating that climate change could reduce global GDP by 7% by 2100. In contrast, investing in renewable energy and reducing greenhouse gas emissions could create up to 46 million new jobs globally by 2050, according to the International Renewable Energy Agency (IRENA).

However, opponents argue that prioritizing climate change may divert resources away from other pressing issues, such as poverty, hunger, and access to healthcare. In 2020, an estimated 736 million people lived in extreme poverty, and 820 million suffered from hunger, according to the World Bank and the United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). Additionally, the World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that 400 million people lack access to essential healthcare services. Critics also argue that the costs of transitioning to a low-carbon economy may be too high for some countries, with the estimated cost of achieving the Paris Agreement's goals ranging from $1.6 trillion to $3.8 trillion per year. Nevertheless, proponents counter that climate change disproportionately affects vulnerable populations, including the poor and marginalized, and that addressing it is essential for achieving the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and ensuring a sustainable future for all.
