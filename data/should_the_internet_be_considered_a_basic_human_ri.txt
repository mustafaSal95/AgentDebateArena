Topic: Should the internet be considered a basic human right?

The debate on whether the internet should be considered a basic human right is a contentious issue. Proponents argue that internet access is essential for modern life, enabling individuals to exercise their rights to freedom of expression, education, and access to information. According to the United Nations, 4.1 billion people, or 53.6% of the global population, had internet access in 2019, with the number increasing by 1,000 people per minute. The internet has been recognized as a key driver of economic growth, with a study by the McKinsey Global Institute finding that every 10% increase in broadband penetration results in a 1.4% increase in GDP growth. Furthermore, the internet has been instrumental in promoting social and political change, with 71% of online adults using the internet to stay informed about current events.

Opponents, however, argue that the internet is a privilege, not a right, and that its provision is subject to economic and infrastructural constraints. In 2020, an estimated 3.8 billion people, or 46.4% of the global population, lacked internet access, with the majority residing in developing countries. The cost of providing universal internet access is substantial, with estimates suggesting that it would require an investment of $450 billion to $1.2 trillion. Additionally, some argue that the internet can also be a tool for oppression, with 71 countries having implemented internet shutdowns or restrictions since 2015, affecting 1.8 billion people. The Special Rapporteur on the promotion and protection of the right to freedom of opinion and expression has acknowledged these concerns, emphasizing the need for a nuanced approach that balances the benefits of internet access with the potential risks and challenges.
