Topic: Can nuclear energy be a safe and efficient source of power?

Nuclear energy is a highly debated topic, with proponents arguing it can be a safe and efficient source of power. According to the World Nuclear Association, nuclear power plants generate approximately 10% of the world's electricity, with 440 operational reactors in 30 countries. The nuclear industry has a strong safety record, with a fatality rate of 0.01 deaths per terawatt-hour (TWh) of electricity generated, compared to 0.24 deaths/TWh for fossil fuels and 0.45 deaths/TWh for hydropower. Additionally, nuclear power plants produce virtually no greenhouse gas emissions during operation, with the average reactor emitting 2 grams of CO2 equivalent per kilowatt-hour (kWh) of electricity generated. The European Nuclear Society estimates that nuclear energy can reduce CO2 emissions by up to 2.2 billion tons per year, equivalent to taking 400 million cars off the road.

However, opponents argue that nuclear energy poses significant safety risks, citing the Chernobyl and Fukushima Daiichi disasters, which released large quantities of radioactive material into the environment. The estimated cost of the Fukushima cleanup is approximately $200 billion, with the surrounding area still largely uninhabitable. Furthermore, the storage and disposal of nuclear waste remain unresolved issues, with the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission estimating that the country's nuclear waste inventory will reach 140,000 tons by 2050. Despite these concerns, the International Energy Agency (IEA) projects that nuclear energy will play a crucial role in meeting global energy demands, with an estimated 17% increase in nuclear capacity by 2030. The IEA also notes that next-generation nuclear reactors, such as small modular reactors (SMRs) and integral pressurized water reactors (iPWRs), offer improved safety features and reduced waste production, potentially mitigating some of the concerns surrounding nuclear energy.
