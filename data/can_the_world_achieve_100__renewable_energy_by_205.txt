Topic: Can the world achieve 100% renewable energy by 2050?

The prospect of achieving 100% renewable energy by 2050 is a highly debated topic. Proponents argue that rapid advancements in technology and declining costs make this goal feasible. According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), solar energy costs have fallen by 73% over the past decade, while wind energy costs have decreased by 69%. Additionally, the global renewable energy capacity has grown from 1,200 gigawatts (GW) in 2010 to over 3,300 GW in 2022, with solar and wind energy accounting for 83% of this growth. The National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL) estimates that widespread adoption of renewable energy could reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 78% and create up to 24 million new jobs globally by 2050.

However, critics argue that achieving 100% renewable energy by 2050 is unrealistic due to limitations in energy storage, grid infrastructure, and land use requirements. For instance, a study by the University of California, Berkeley found that achieving 100% renewable energy in the United States would require a land area equivalent to 15% of the country's total landmass. Furthermore, the IEA estimates that meeting global energy demands with 100% renewable energy would require an investment of $1.7 trillion per year from 2020 to 2050. In contrast, the current global investment in renewable energy is around $300 billion per year. Despite these challenges, many countries and companies are making significant strides towards achieving 100% renewable energy, with Norway, Costa Rica, and Google already reaching or exceeding 90% renewable energy targets.
