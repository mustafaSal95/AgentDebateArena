Topic: Should the internet be considered a fundamental human right?

The debate on whether the internet should be considered a fundamental human right is a contentious issue. Proponents argue that internet access is essential for the realization of various human rights, including freedom of expression, education, and access to information. According to the United Nations, over 4.1 billion people, or 54% of the global population, have access to the internet, with 93% of the world's population living within range of a mobile cellular network. The internet has become a critical tool for socio-economic development, with online access to education, healthcare, and financial services. For instance, online education platforms have increased access to education, with over 200 million students worldwide using online learning platforms. Furthermore, the internet has enabled the creation of new job opportunities, with the global digital economy valued at over $11.5 trillion.

However, opponents argue that considering the internet a fundamental human right is premature, given the existing digital divide and unequal access to internet services. In 2020, only 35% of the population in developing countries had access to the internet, compared to 80% in developed countries. Additionally, the cost of internet access remains a significant barrier, with the average cost of a broadband connection exceeding 40% of the monthly income of an individual in many developing countries. Moreover, the internet is not a traditional human right, as it is a relatively new technology that has evolved rapidly over the past few decades. The International Telecommunication Union estimates that achieving universal and affordable internet access will require an investment of over $450 billion by 2025. Despite these challenges, the importance of internet access for human development and social inclusion is undeniable, with 80% of households with internet access reporting improved access to information, education, and healthcare services.
