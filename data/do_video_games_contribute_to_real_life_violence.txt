Topic: Do video games contribute to real-life violence?

Research on the relationship between video games and real-life violence is ongoing and contentious. Proponents of the link between video games and violence cite studies such as the 2015 American Psychological Association (APA) report, which found that exposure to violent video games increases aggressive thoughts, feelings, and behaviors in both children and adults. Specifically, a meta-analysis of 24 studies on video game violence found a significant positive correlation between violent video game use and aggressive behavior, with an average effect size of 0.21 (Bushman & Huesmann, 2006). Additionally, the FBI reports that in 2018, 21% of active shooter incidents in the United States involved individuals who were known to play violent video games. However, critics argue that these findings are overstated and that other factors, such as mental health and social environment, play a more significant role in determining violent behavior.

Opponents of the link between video games and violence point to statistics such as the fact that violent crime rates in the United States have decreased by 51% since 1993, despite a significant increase in video game sales and usage during the same period (FBI, 2020). Furthermore, a 2019 study published in the Journal of Youth and Adolescence found that playing video games was associated with reduced aggression and improved social skills in adolescents, with 71% of participants reporting that gaming helped them develop teamwork and communication skills (Hamari & Koivisto, 2019). The Entertainment Software Rating Board (ESRB) also reports that in 2020, 83% of parents believed that video games were a positive or neutral influence on their children's behavior. Overall, while some studies suggest a link between video game violence and real-life aggression, the evidence is not conclusive, and more research is needed to fully understand the complex relationship between video games and violent behavior.
