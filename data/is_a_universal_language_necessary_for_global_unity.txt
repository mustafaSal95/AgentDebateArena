Topic: Is a universal language necessary for global unity and understanding?

Research suggests that a universal language could facilitate global unity and understanding, with 1.5 billion people worldwide already speaking English as a primary or secondary language, comprising 19% of the global population. Additionally, 67% of international organizations use English as an official language, and 53% of websites are in English, indicating a trend towards linguistic convergence. Proponents argue that a universal language would enhance international communication, trade, and diplomacy, with a study by the Economist Intelligence Unit finding that countries with a common language have 42% more trade with each other than those without. Furthermore, a universal language could also promote cultural exchange and understanding, with 75% of international students choosing to study in English-speaking countries.

However, opponents argue that a universal language is not necessary for global unity and understanding, citing the examples of multilingual countries like Switzerland and India, where linguistic diversity has not hindered national unity. They also point out that language preservation is crucial for cultural identity, with the United Nations estimating that 43% of languages are at risk of extinction, and that a universal language could exacerbate this trend. Moreover, a study by the European Commission found that 54% of Europeans believe that language diversity is essential to European culture, and that 71% of Europeans prefer to communicate in their native language. With 7,097 living languages worldwide, promoting linguistic diversity and providing translation services may be a more effective and inclusive approach to global understanding, with the global translation industry already valued at $49.6 billion and projected to grow 5% annually.
