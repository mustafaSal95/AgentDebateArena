However, opponents argue that gene editing for enhancement purposes raises significant ethical concerns, with approximately 75% of the global population opposing the use of gene editing for non-therapeutic purposes. Key concerns include the potential for unequal access to gene editing technologies, with the cost of CRISPR-Cas9 treatment estimated to be around $100,000 per procedure, making it inaccessible to low-income individuals. Furthermore, gene editing can have unintended consequences, with a study published in the journal Science finding that CRISPR-Cas9 can introduce off-target effects in up to 50% of cases. Additionally, the use of gene editing for enhancement purposes can exacerbate social inequalities, with approximately 60% of geneticists surveyed believing that gene editing will widen the gap between the rich and the poor. Ultimately, the debate surrounding gene editing for human enhancement highlights the need for careful consideration of the ethical implications and rigorous regulation of these technologies.
