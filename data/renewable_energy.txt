Renewable Energy Overview

Solar photovoltaic costs have fallen roughly 90% over the past decade, making
solar the cheapest source of new electricity generation in many regions.
Wind power costs have also dropped significantly, though less steeply than solar.

Grid storage remains the primary bottleneck for renewable adoption at scale.
Lithium-ion battery costs have declined but grid-scale storage is still
expensive relative to fossil-fuel peaker plants for handling demand spikes.

Nuclear power provides consistent baseload generation with a small land
footprint, but new plant construction times average 7-10 years in most
Western countries, with frequent cost overruns.

Fossil fuels still supply roughly 60% of global electricity generation as of
recent estimates, with coal being the single largest source of energy-related
carbon emissions worldwide.
