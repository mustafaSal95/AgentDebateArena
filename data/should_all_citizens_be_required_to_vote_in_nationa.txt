Topic: Should all citizens be required to vote in national elections?

Compulsory voting in national elections is a debated topic, with proponents arguing it increases civic engagement and political legitimacy. In countries with mandatory voting, such as Australia and Belgium, voter turnout is significantly higher, with 91.9% and 89.2% turnout rates, respectively, in their most recent federal elections. In contrast, the United States, which does not have compulsory voting, had a turnout rate of 63.1% in the 2020 presidential election. Advocates for compulsory voting argue that it reduces inequality in voter participation, as lower-income and minority groups are more likely to be disenfranchised. For example, in the 2020 US election, 70% of voters from households with incomes above $100,000 cast ballots, compared to 46% from households with incomes below $30,000.

Opponents of compulsory voting argue that it infringes upon individual freedom and may lead to uninformed voting. In the United States, for instance, a Gallup poll found that 35% of respondents believed that compulsory voting would lead to more uninformed voters. Additionally, some argue that mandatory voting may not necessarily lead to more informed or engaged citizens, as 22% of Australian voters, where voting is compulsory, reported not being interested in politics. Furthermore, implementing compulsory voting would require significant resources, with estimated costs ranging from $200 million to $1 billion in the United States. Despite these concerns, countries with compulsory voting have seen increased political stability and satisfaction with democracy, with 77% of Belgians and 73% of Australians reporting satisfaction with their democratic systems, compared to 55% in the United States.
